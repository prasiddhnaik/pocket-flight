F-15C Claude reference audit

23 October 2026 · Research only · Reference dossier · Structured findings

The article contains useful reference numbers, but its promise of an exact wing reconstruction is stronger than the evidence. The published wing reference is about 56.5 m² with a 4.86 m mean aerodynamic chord. These numbers constrain an ideal reference wing; they do not specify every exposed production-C edge. Its proposed game flight model is a starting point: aerodynamic derivatives, engine ratings and research-aircraft mass properties must retain their conditions.

Wing interpretation and current model

For a straight-edged trapezoid extending to the centerline, S = 56.5 m², b = 13.05 m and taper = 0.25 give root chord 6.927 m, tip chord 1.732 m, MAC 4.849 m and AR 3.014. Using TM-72861’s 56.61 m² gives MAC 4.858 m, essentially its tabulated 4.86 m. This is a useful consistency check, not proof that a production wing is exactly that trapezoid. TM-72861, printed p. 15 / PDF p. 17.

Findings and conditions for use

The rows distinguish verified article claims, explicit wording that needs qualification, additional conditions for applying the reference, and an independent comparison with the current model. The article already labels its named-airfoil source, flap/anhedral conflicts, local lift slope, inertia estimates, modified-F-15B transfer and worked turn outputs; those cautions are preserved here.

A01 · S ≈ 56.5 m², b ≈ 13.05 m, taper 0.25, leading-edge sweep 45°, MAC ≈ 4.86 m
Verified article data
Supported as reference geometry, with small source differences

Article position: Section 3.1 lists the nominal values and explicitly records TM-72861’s 56.61-m² alternative.

TM-72861 Table 1 gives reference S = 56.61 m², b = 13.05 m, taper = 0.25, LE = 45° and MAC = 4.86 m. TM-4604 Table 1 gives 608 ft² = 56.485 m² and 42.83 ft. CAP gives AR = 3.01, taper = 0.25 and LE = 45°. The article correctly reports these small source differences.

TM72861 · TM4604 · CAP		Use a declared nominal reference basis and retain the article’s source labels. Rounded reference values are not a complete production drawing.
A02 · The opening says the wing parameters permit an exact planform reconstruction
Article wording overstatement
Opening overstates what the later recipe establishes

Article position: The opening says the parameters let the reader rebuild the planform exactly. Sections 1 and 3.2 subsequently call it an idealized reference wing and direct the reader to trace the clipped/raked production tip.

The calculation correctly determines a straight-edged reference trapezoid. The article itself later distinguishes that idealization from the cropped production tip. CAP figures 1–3 also distinguish local root/body outline; p. 102 models wing, wing-glove, body and inlets as separate contributors. The parameters alone do not determine those details.

CAP · TM72861		Read the article’s trapezoid as a reference construction, then trace the exposed production outline and body intersection. Qualify the opening’s word “exactly.”
A03 · Current modeled S = 61.560225 m² and MAC = 5.250250 m versus the nominal published reference
Current-model comparison
Unresolved geometric discrepancy in the current model

Article position: The article does not describe or endorse the current project’s larger modeled area; this is an independent comparison.

Current station integration is 8.96% above 56.5 m²; its MAC is 8.03% above 4.86 m. Source code adds 0.65 m chord near the exposed root, tapering toward 0.15 m at the tip, then crops the outer tip. No primary station data reviewed here validates those additions. A different area convention can explain some disagreement, but has not been quantified against the production outline.

CAP · TM72861		Retain current values as measured model values. Establish reference area, exposed planform area, root/glove treatment and exact continuation before deciding which geometry to revise.
A04 · Named root/tip airfoils: NACA 64A006.6 and NACA 64A203
Evidence limit / application caveat
Secondary attribution is explicit; exact production-C ordinates remain provisional

Article position: The article explicitly attributes NACA 64A006.6 and 64A203 to Lednicer’s secondary compilation and separately quotes NASA’s qualitative section descriptions.

CAP p. 100 describes a 6%-thick symmetric root, a 3%-thick highly cambered tip and conical camber outboard of 20% semispan. It does not name those NACA sections. This supports the article’s qualitative comparison but independently confirms neither the exact root 6.6% thickness nor a production-C ordinate set. The current analytic polynomial is also not verified 64A-series geometry.

CAP		Preserve the secondary attribution. Use the named sections as provisional reconstruction inputs, while labeling the current analytic sections as approximations.
A05 · Wing −1° anhedral, 0° incidence and 0° geometric twist
Verified article data with recorded conflict
Supported baseline; conflicting research-aircraft description exists

Article position: The article explicitly flags −1° versus 0° and recommends −1° from TM-72861 and CAP-TSD.

TM-72861 p. 15 gives −1° dihedral, 0° incidence and 0° geometric twist; CAP p. 99 independently gives −1° dihedral. TP-3627 p. 12 instead describes 0° dihedral on its research aircraft. The article correctly preserves this discrepancy.

TM72861 · CAP · TP3627		The article’s selected −1° baseline has direct support. Keep the conflict and production-C photo comparison visible.
A06 · Ailerons ±20°, stabilators +15°/−26°, rudders ±30°; differential stabilator ratio 0.3 per aileron degree
Verified article data / use conditions
Supported for the cited test configuration

Article position: The article gives mechanical limits and the differential stabilator gearing; some additional pedal-authority details are explicitly sourced to secondary simulation references.

TM-72861 Table 1 pp. 15–16 lists the deflection limits. Its appendix p. 12 gives the 0.3° differential-stabilator/aileron-degree gearing, except where actuator-limited. These are surface limits and scheduled system behavior, not a constant stick-to-angle rule across the envelope.

TM72861		Use sign conventions explicitly. Do not equate mechanical rudder travel, pedal authority, CAS authority or ARI authority.
A07 · The article records the 30°/40° flap conflict and recommends 30°
Verified article conflict / application caveat
Conflict is correctly reported; selected production-C travel is still provisional

Article position: The article explicitly flags 30° versus 40° on the NASA airplane and chooses 30° for its proposed build.

TM-4604 p. 5 states a maximum downward 30° and its flight procedure uses 30°. TP-3627 pp. 12–13 states electric flaps with 0°/40° positions on the NASA Dryden aircraft. Both describe modified preproduction research aircraft. These sources confirm the conflict already acknowledged by the article, rather than proving interchangeability.

TM4604 · TP3627		Retain 30° as an explicitly selected modeling assumption until the intended production configuration is checked. Do not characterize the article as saying 30° and 40° are interchangeable.
A08 · Tail dimensions, inferred total/per-fin area and the flagged rudder-area conflict
Verified table values with unresolved conventions
The article appropriately flags ambiguity; do not resolve it by assumption

Article position: The article lists the NASA tail values, marks the per-fin interpretation as inferred and explicitly flags NASA/JSBSim area disagreements and the implausibly large rudder area.

TM-72861 p. 15 supports stabilator total area 11.15 m², span 8.61 m, exposed root chord 3.48 m, tip 1.18 m and LE sweep 50°. Its fin/rudder table has an unstated total/per-fin convention and a 6.07-m² total rudder area that is questionable beside the listed outline. The article already identifies those issues.

TM72861 · CAP		Keep the article’s inference and conflict labels. Use the outline and explicitly defined exposed chords; do not promote the suspect table entry to a precise CAD target.
A09 · CD0 = 0.0218 is a NASA F-15C flight-data value
Verified article datum / added condition detail
Supported, conditional

Article position: The article labels CD0 = 0.0218 as subsonic NASA flight data and separately calls its transonic multipliers estimates.

TP-3414 Table 5 is on printed p. 14, PDF p. 18, and gives F-15C CD0 = 0.0218. The surrounding definition is subsonic, trimmed, nonlifting aerodynamic efficiency. Printed p. 11 says the F-15C/F-16C comparison data are from unpublished flight tests. The table does not expose a full F-15C Mach/altitude/store-state record.

TP3414		Use as a conditional comparison datum. Do not treat it as a universal constant, apply the X-29A test range to the F-15C, or claim it validates the current aircraft polar.
A10 · CLα = 0.065/deg = 3.72/rad at α = 8°, from the NASA simulator database
Verified article datum / transfer caveat
Article’s value and local attribution are supported

Article position: The article explicitly labels CLα = 0.065/deg as an α = 8° value from the NASA Dryden simulator database.

TM-4604 p. 8 confirms this derivative and adds nominal stabilator = −5°. The derivatives correct landing-approach data for unwanted attitude/control changes; the method assumes they are locally constant and unchanged by ground effect. Aircraft and approach configuration are given on pp. 5–6. This extends the article’s condition label; it does not refute it.

TM4604		Preserve the article’s α = 8° and simulator labels, plus the stabilator/approach conditions. Application caveat: do not use the number as a direct isolated-wing VLM validation target.
A11 · Companion CLδ, Cmα and Cmδ values from TM-4604
Verified article data / transfer caveat
Quoted numbers supported; preserve their local use conditions

Article position: The article lists the companion stabilator-lift and pitching-moment derivatives from TM-4604 alongside its explicitly local CLα datum.

The quoted 0.005/deg, −0.0021/deg and −0.00072/deg occur in the same local approach-correction method as CLα on TM-4604 p. 8. The paper defines its own stabilator sign convention. The article does not explicitly claim these values hold throughout the flight envelope.

TM4604		When applying them, retain units, signs and approach conditions. A high-angle or transonic extension would require additional evidence; this is an application limit, not a claim the article makes.
A12 · F100-PW-220 maximum afterburner thrust 23,770 lbf ≈ 105.7 kN per engine
Verified article rating / remaining verification gap
Supported rating, not an installed flight map

Article position: The article lists PW-220 dry/afterburner ratings, distinguishes engine variants and labels its altitude map an engineering approximation.

NMUSAF publishes 23,770 lbf maximum afterburner thrust. The 14,590-lbf dry rating was not independently primary-verified in this pass. Current manufacturer F100 specifications emphasize the stronger PW-229; that is a different variant.

NMUSAF220 · PW		The current twin 210-kN afterburner estimate is close to twice the museum rating. Keep installation, altitude, speed and throttle mapping estimated rather than automatically increasing it.
A13 · “Static thrust is conservative because ram effect adds thrust at speed” in the sustained-turn example
Article physical inference requiring correction
The stated justification is incomplete

Article position: Worked example B states that static thrust is conservative because ram effect adds thrust at speed.

Net propulsive force depends on gross exhaust thrust minus inlet momentum/ram drag and installation effects. TP-3627 p. 10 explicitly discusses gross thrust minus ram drag in its low-speed research context. Added ram pressure alone does not establish that a static rating is conservative at the example’s speed.

TP3627		Keep the static-thrust example labeled as an approximation. Remove the guaranteed-conservatism rationale or support it with a PW-220 installed net-thrust map for that condition.
A14 · JSBSim inertia values are estimates; a measured production-C inertia set was not found
Additional source / estimate-transfer caveat
Estimate label is appropriate; research-aircraft curves add context without proving a measured C set

Article position: The article explicitly labels the JSBSim inertias as estimates. Its final caveat says a measured inertia set was not found in primary public sources.

TP-3627 pp. 18–19 publishes fuel-dependent moments and product of inertia in Figure 14 for NASA aircraft number 8. It states that each research engine is about 400 lb heavier than standard, with compensating nose ballast, increasing pitch/yaw inertia. This is additional primary mass-property information, but the cited passage does not establish a measured production-C inertia set or refute the article’s qualified search result.

TP3627		Retain the article’s estimate label. Use the NASA curves only with their different-airframe/configuration qualification; neither those curves nor the JSBSim estimates are production-C ground truth.
A15 · Modified-F-15B derivative transfer and the wing quarter-chord “balance point” explanation
Transfer caveat and article simplification
Configuration warnings are appropriate; the balance-point explanation is too broad

Article position: The article warns that lateral derivatives are from a canard-equipped F-15B and approximate for a C; it labels CG data as NASA test-aircraft data. Separately, its first-principles visual tip calls the wing MAC quarter-chord the aircraft’s aerodynamic balance point.

TM-2003-212027 pp. 4–5 confirms the modified F-15B with canards, PW-229 engines and special nozzles. TP-3627 pp. 18–20 gives its own research fuel/CG schedule. These support the article’s existing cautions. The quarter-chord of an ideal wing does not by itself establish the full-aircraft neutral point, moment-reference datum or CG target.

TM212027 · TP3627		Preserve the article’s test-configuration labels. Treat the wing quarter-chord as a wing reference marker, not a demonstrated complete-aircraft balance point.
A16 · Worked corner-speed and sustained-turn examples under declared assumptions
Article worked example / application caveat
Fairly labeled model outputs; independent performance validation remains open

Article position: The article explicitly calls the turn results model outputs, not F-15 manual values, and discloses static thrust, assumed CLmax = 1.0 and assumed efficiency.

The article’s approximately 441-kt corner speed and 18.9°/s sustained turn depend on the disclosed mass, CLmax, efficiency, drag polar, static thrust and atmosphere. The cited NASA reports do not independently validate that complete combination, and the article does not claim the outputs are manual values.

TP3414 · TM4604		Retain the article’s model-output label. Address the thrust rationale in A13 and seek condition-matched performance evidence before treating the examples as calibration targets.
A17 · The gun is in the right wing root; photographs refine its visible shoulder location
Verified article location / photographic refinement
Supported qualitative location

Article position: The article places the M61A1 in the right wing root and treats this section as exterior modeling.

The user’s closed-port photo and official NMUSAF production-C photographs show the recessed oval aperture in the outer right wing-root shoulder, outboard of the inlet’s upper outer region. It is not centered on the movable intake roof.

PHOTO		Retain the corrected one-sided exterior location and shallow oval port. Do not infer exact manufacturing stations from a perspective photograph.
A18 · Generic Blender −Y nose to glTF +Z recipe versus this project’s existing axes
Project-specific application caveat
Adaptation required for this project; not an error in the generic recipe

Article position: The article gives a generic Blender nose −Y to glTF +Z workflow; it does not claim this is the current project’s CAD convention.

The article describes a generic Blender/glTF convention. This project’s current CAD authoring nose is +Y, and runtime axes are +X right, +Y up and +Z aft. The runtime geometry file declares its conversion and ground-datum subtraction. A valid generic recipe should not replace the current exporter’s established transform.

Current local source		Keep the distinction explicit when adapting the article. Verify exported muzzle, controls and camera axes through the actual project pipeline.

Coefficient normalization is separate from changing physics

Lift and drag are qSC. If only the coefficient reference area changes, preserve dimensional forces with Cnew = Cold × Sold / Snew. For a pitch-moment coefficient, also include the reference-chord ratio. A change from the current 61.560225 m² and CD0 = 0.026 to 56.5 m² and CD0 = 0.0218 changes zero-lift drag area from 1.600566 to 1.231700 m², about a 23% reduction. That is a physical retuning, not merely a label correction.

Similarly, current S × CLα is about 180.18 m²/rad; the article’s 56.5 × 3.724 is about 210.42 m²/rad. These different dimensional slopes are not directly comparable validation results because the first is a wing-only attached-flow fit and the second is a local full-aircraft approach database value. Do not transfer coefficients without preserving the area, configuration and test condition. TM-4604, printed p. 8 / PDF p. 12; TP-3414, printed p. 14 / PDF p. 18.
