F-15C outer skin in Blender, gun and missile animations

1 November 2026 · Pocket Flight · Waiting for approval · Nothing here is built yet

Why

The user wants the F-15C to look right. Its sills looked "like an extrusion", and the overall goal is an Ace Combat 6 look. A comparison against reference images (assets/f15c/review/reference/) shows the size is correct but the shape is not: the body is too narrow and blended, the intakes are wedges, the canopy is a bubble on a thin nose, and the wings and tail are misplaced.

The barrel animation also looks wrong: at 60 fps the barrels appear to spin backward, and they reach full speed almost at once. Missiles have no launch animation at all; they appear at the pylon and fly away.

What gets built

  1. New outer skin, modelled in Blender against the references.
  2. New gun barrel firing animation, made in Blender.
  3. Missile launch animation, with detailed missiles made in Blender.
  4. The reference comparison re-run on the new skin, so the improvement is measured.

Not part of this

  • Flight handling. It does not change.
  • Landing gear parts, cockpit interior, pilot, engine nozzle parts and gun barrels. They stay in CAD and only move where the new skin requires.
  • Other aircraft's skins, a new paint scheme, bomb release, and the Palm Bay map and missions.

This fails if

  • Parts look laid on top of the body rather than part of it, or any part floats.
  • The barrels still look like they spin backward, or the gun feels laggy to fire.
  • The plane flies differently, or the gear, moving surfaces, replays or sandbox break.

The new skin

How it is made

  • A new script, scripts/build-f15c-skin.py, builds a subdivision-surface model and saves assets/f15c-skin.blend, so it can also be sculpted by hand. It is mirrored left to right, except for the gun side.
  • Body, intakes and wing roots are one continuous surface. Moving surfaces stay separate, with their hinge points, axes and travel preserved in the export.
  • The script imports the CAD mechanical parts, moves them to fit, and exports the game model.
  • scripts/prepare-f15c.ts now writes straight to public/assets/f15c-eagle.glb. It must write to an intermediate CAD file instead, or the next CAD build would overwrite the Blender skin.
  • The old CAD skin stays in the repository, unused.

Gun barrels

  • A new motion-blurred barrel cylinder takes over from the individual barrels at speed, which removes the backward spin.
  • Spin-up takes about a third of a second. Wind-down takes about a second, with smoke drifting from the muzzle.
  • A small shake at the muzzle and a flash while firing.
  • Blender holds the curves and the parts as clips in the model. The game sets the clip time from the trigger and the recorded replay values, so live play and Replay Studio always match.

Missile launch

  • Detailed AIM-9 and AIM-120 models with a motor plume, made in Blender, replace the code-built missiles and the stand-ins in the sandbox.
  • Rail launch (wing pylon): motor lights, the missile slides off the rail, and fins twitch as it steers.
  • Drop launch (body stations): the missile is pushed down clear of the aircraft, falls briefly, then lights.
  • The clip lasts under a second, then blends into the missile's simulated flight, so guidance and hit results do not change.
  • Only the fired missile leaves its station. The others stay visible.
  • Replays record each round's age, so the launch plays in Replay Studio. Older recordings keep working without the launch clip.

Order of work

Each step ends with images for the user. Nothing is committed unless asked.

  1. Blockout. Main shapes only, measured against the references, in Blender. The user approves the shape before any detail. This is the riskiest step and costs the least to redo.
  2. Detailed skin. Panel lines, intake mouths and ducts, wells, moving surfaces.
  3. In the game. Export, collision, hinges, gear, stores, paint. The plane must fly and behave as before.
  4. Gun animation. After the skin, because the gun port moves.
  5. Missile launch.
  6. Final check. Comparison re-run, full tests, Replay Studio.

Risks

  • Size of the job. This is the largest item so far. The blockout step limits how much is lost if the shape is wrong.
  • Mechanical parts may not fit. A wider body and moved wings could force gear or cockpit changes in CAD, which rebuilds slowly (about 8 minutes from the cockpit stage).
  • Visible wing and flight wing will differ. Accepted, to protect handling. docs/f15c/F15C_CLAUDE_REFERENCE_AUDIT.html already records the current wing area as about 9% above published figures.
  • The drawing is an F-15A by one author. Overlap targets are a guide; the photos and the user decide.
