Combat range: design and first playable slice

31 October 2026. Provisional gameplay choices by the implementation agent. This design was written before implementation and updated to describe the resulting slice. The game designer has not approved the choices below.

Loop and scope

Select a fighter or Finch A–2, then choose Combat range beside Start flight and Free flight. Start airborne over open water at (-1700, 160, -1400), heading north, at the selected aircraft’s existing trimmed air-start speed. Engage four marked targets, watch ammunition, hits and destroyed counts, then turn for another pass. Targets return after five seconds. R restarts and rearms; Esc pauses or opens the route back to the menu. There is no victory screen or time limit.

This mode uses its own mission identity and target set. Existing missions, terrain and scenery remain intact. No enemy AI, incoming fire, player combat damage or island redesign is included. Existing crash damage still applies.

Aiming, HUD and accessibility

The existing circular cannon reticle remains a 500 m ballistic reference. A separate + marks boresight for stores; it is not a rocket impact prediction. Targets show AIR or GROUND, distance in metres and health. Orange boxes are surface targets; cyan wire spheres are airborne targets. Lock state, ammunition, hits, destruction and brief impact feedback appear in a combat HUD. Separate pointer controls support gun hold and rocket/missile release. Buttons have text labels and keyboard activation; status uses a polite live region.

Hits, damage and targets

Two airborne and two ground practice targets sit 500–900 m ahead of the start. Their gameplay hit volumes are spheres, seven metres for air and six for ground. Each has three health points. A gun hit removes one point; a rocket or missile removes three. Destroyed targets stop colliding immediately and respawn after five seconds. Multiple rounds cannot count an already destroyed target again. Impacts produce a short expanding flash. Targets are static, have no hostility or effect on flight contact geometry, and exist only in the range.

Data and aircraft integration

Hardpoint = { id: string; position: [number, number, number]; loads: string[] }, in aircraft metres: X right, Y up, -Z forward. A loadout is an array of { hardpoint, store }. The catalog owns store behavior, round count and mass/drag metadata. Validation rejects unknown/incompatible stores, missing or duplicate stations, duplicate hardpoint ids and invalid coordinates. Firing consumes this validated data and transforms the selected station into world space.

Fighter stations are extracted from the existing measured visual store mounts, with model scale and offsets converted into aircraft-frame metres. Their default assignment puts a rocket pod on the first station on each side and training missiles on the remainder. Existing display stores hide while combat visuals are active; launch removes missiles from the corresponding station, and returning to other modes restores the original presentation.

The concurrent Finch project uses aircraft id finch with variant attack; the menu/combat adapter calls it finch-attack. Combat consumes its exported finchAttackHardpoints. Only the compatible outer rocket-pod-7 stations are loaded. Inner bomb stations remain empty: bomb release is deferred. No AircraftId union change or Finch-source edit is required. Future aircraft may call registerCombatAircraft(id, { hardpoints, loadout, gun }); gun can be null.

Mass and drag switch: off

STORE_PHYSICS_ENABLED = false. Store metadata and storePhysicsDelta exist, but no call applies them to FlightPhysics, aircraft mass budgets or aerodynamics. Enabling the metadata calculation alone does not enable flight effects. A separate reviewed integration should add remaining store and ammunition mass to aircraft mass/CG/inertia, use station lever arms, and add store drag in air-relative axes without double-counting mass already present in aircraft budgets. Release must subtract the same mass and moments. Pod shell mass must remain after ammunition is gone. Verify asymmetry, trim, stall, release and takeoff before enabling. Catalog masses and drag areas are provisional and are not calibrated aircraft data.

Lifecycle and verification boundary

Combat state resets on restart and is removed on return to the menu or another mission. Pause, blur and suspension clear weapon input and missile acquisition. Simulation freezes during pause. Shared GPU resources and HUD nodes are disposed on reset/teardown. Flight replay remains aircraft playback: target state, ordnance and damage events are not recorded, and the original display stores appear during replay. Combat replay recording is deferred.

Core tests cover loadout compatibility, pose transforms, finite inventory, gravity/frame-rate agreement, nearest swept hits, terrain occlusion, damage/respawn, lock rules, missile hits and visual cleanup. Browser checks use actual keyboard events and the running game loop after setting an explicit aiming pose. They establish functional integration, not unassisted pilot usability, touch-device visual acceptance or production verification.

My call — every provisional gameplay choice

  1. Open-ended training sandbox, separate launch button, no score or victory screen.
  2. Air start over open water at the coordinates above; existing trim speed and flight handling.
  3. Fighters and attack Finch may enter; civilian/trainer variants cannot.
  4. Two static air and two ground targets, 500–900 m ahead; no AI or incoming fire.
  5. Three health points; gun damage one, ordnance damage three; five-second respawn; hits and destruction tracked separately.
  6. Spherical gameplay hit volumes, seven metres air/six ground; no area damage or blast radius.
  7. J holds gun, K releases rocket, L releases missile; separate on-screen buttons; one store release per press.
  8. Two four-round rocket pods per fighter and two seven-round pods on Finch; remaining fighter stations carry training missiles. Restart rearms everything.
  9. Fighter stations use measured visual mounts; all accept training rocket pods or air-to-air missiles. Finch accepts only its declared compatible loads.
  10. Keep the F-15C gun’s original ballistics/capacity. Other fighters temporarily share those values at provisional gun ports; Finch has no gun.
  11. Rocket 350 m/s plus aircraft velocity, gravity only, eight-second life; no wind/drag/burn model.
  12. Missile 520 m/s plus aircraft velocity, bounded 35°/s pursuit, twelve-second life, no gravity or speed decay.
  13. Missile nearest-air-target auto-selection, 12° cone, 30–2,500 m range, 0.6 s uninterrupted lock; no ground lock, countermeasures or retargeting.
  14. Gun’s existing 500 m reticle plus a store boresight; no predicted rocket-impact cue.
  15. Orange ground boxes, cyan air wire spheres, explicit labels/health/distance and short hit/destruction feedback.
  16. Hide display-only missiles and replace them with loadout-driven combat stores while in the range. Empty rocket pods stay attached.
  17. Keep bomb stations empty until bomb release is implemented.
  18. Store mass/drag remain off; no player combat damage or weapons replay recording in this slice.
